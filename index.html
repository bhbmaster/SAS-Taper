SAS-Taper

GitHub: bhbmaster/SAS-Taper

SAS means save-a-sliver. Each day you cut 1/n off the current piece, save that sliver, and take the rest, treating what remains as a complete strip. After n days the save jar holds one full piece: a buffer, not extra dose. Numbers update as you change the inputs.

Not medical advice. This is a calculator for a plan to take to your prescriber. Buprenorphine pieces are severely dangerous to children and pets. Keep saved slivers locked up. Holding a cycle is fine; cravings are the signal that matters most.

How to use this

Why save-a-sliver

The hard part of a taper is often not the milligrams but the sense of getting less. SAS frames each day's remaining piece as a whole strip, the dose you have, while the cut-off sliver leaves the daily routine. People work with what is in hand. Surplus that stays in view is easier to use, and once the sliver is saved it is no longer part of today's dose. The bank is a safety net if you need to step back up, not a second supply for the same day.

Instructions

  1. Use the calculator on this page, or run the same math in a terminal with python3 taper.py from the repo. To keep a copy offline, save this page. It is one self-contained file and needs no connection.
  2. Pick a cut mode. Geometric (the default) cuts the same fraction every cycle, so the percentage step holds steady and the ladder never quite reaches zero. Linear cuts the same amount every cycle, so the milligram step holds steady and the ladder lands on zero after about n of them. Linear is easier to cut, because it is the same mark every day rather than a new smaller one each cycle. The trade is a steeper finish. You can switch and compare; nothing else changes.
  3. Measure your film with a ruler along its length only, the 22 mm side, the one you will be cutting. Put that number in "Start film length". All four Suboxone strengths are 22 mm on that side, so it is the same number whichever you start on. Default n = 6 (16.7% every 6 days). If you want a gentler taper, raise n to 8 or 10.
  4. The cards, graphs, and schedule recalculate live. Click a cycle row, or a graph step, to see that day's cut mark and a life-size film: TAKE on the left, SAVE on the right. The cut-mark bar is a full unused film; later cycles hash the rest that is already off.
  5. Standing at the counter you need four numbers, and the schedule's tinted block is exactly those four plus one: Take in mg and mm, Save in mg and mm, and Δ save. Open a film, mark it at Take mm from its left end, cut once. Left of the mark is the dose. Everything right of it goes in the jar, all of it, not just this cycle's sliver. Δ save is how much more the jar gets than last cycle, and it grows every cycle. No arithmetic at the strip.
  6. If your dose is bigger than one film, say 16 mg on 8 mg strips, the day is simply several strips. The Film column shows ×2, and only one of those strips is ever cut: take the whole ones as they are.
  7. The life-size film shows Folding by default: the same dose as whole parts of a folded grid. Halve it, halve it again, take five of six. That needs no ruler and no arithmetic, and it is easier to repeat than measuring 15.28 mm at 6 am. It always tells you how far off the ladder that lands, and in linear mode it lands exactly. Switch to Measuring for the exact mark in millimetres; the cut-mark panel above always measures either way. If you want to know how the fold gets picked, there is a page on it.
  8. Print / save as PDF and take it to your prescriber before day 1 so they can match quantity and write 2 mg films for the back half.

What each input does

Defaults in brackets. Only the first three usually need touching; everything else has a sensible default.

Start dose (mg) [8] ·
What you take daily now, before the taper begins. It also decides which film strength the plan is built on: 2, 4, 8 or 12 mg, whichever is the smallest that holds it. Above 12 mg no single film is big enough, so the day becomes several strips: 16 mg is two 8 mg films, and only one of them gets cut.
n (days & cut) [6] ·
Does two jobs at once, which is the whole trick: you cut 1/n off the piece each day, and each cycle lasts n days. After n days the saved slivers add up to one whole piece. n = 6 drops 16.7% per cycle; n = 8 drops 12.5%; n = 10 drops 10%. Bigger n means a gentler, slower taper.
Start film length (mm) [22] ·
Measure your own film along the side you will cut and put that number here. All four Suboxone strengths are 22 mm on that side, so the default is usually right. Measure anyway, because every cut mark on the page comes from it.
2 mg film length (mm) [22] ·
The same measurement for the 2 mg film, used after the switch. Ignored if you turn the switch off.
Target (mg/day) [1] ·
Where the ladder stops. The run ends on the first cycle at or below this dose. Must be lower than the start dose.
Strip strength (mg) [8] ·
The strength of one film as dispensed. Used only to convert milligrams into "how many strips", for the totals and the prescription-quantity table. It does not change the ladder.
Cut mode [geometric] ·
The two shapes a taper can have. Geometric (the default) cuts 1/n off the piece in your hand, so the cut shrinks along with the dose and every cycle is the same percentage step, a steady 16.7% at n = 6. The dose keeps halving and never quite reaches zero, so you pick a target and stop there. Linear cuts 1/n of your first strip and then that identical piece, same milligrams and same millimetres, every cycle after. The dose falls in equal steps and lands on zero after n − 1 of them: 8 mg at n = 6 goes 6.67, 5.33, 4.00, 2.67, 1.33, then nothing. Linear is the easier one to actually cut: one mark, measured once, reused every single day, instead of a new and smaller measurement every cycle, and it never shrinks into the sub-millimetre range where a razor stops being accurate. It is also much shorter and uses much less medication. What it costs is the shape of the finish: equal milligrams mean the percentage grows every cycle: 20%, then 25%, 33%, 50%, and a final 100%. The page shows you those numbers the moment you switch.
Film strength you cut (mg) [blank = auto] ·
Which strength of film you actually hold. Left blank, the tool picks the smallest official size that fits the start dose (2, 4, 8 or 12 mg), and 8 mg for a start above 12 mg, where no single film holds the dose. Set it if you have something else: a 16 mg start is two 8 mg strips a day, or two 12 mg strips if that is what is in the box.
Hold / stretch days [blank = same as n] ·
Makes every cycle longer without changing the size of the cut. Set 9 with n = 6 and you still cut 1/6, but hold each dose for 9 days instead of 6. Same taper, more settling time between drops.
n below 3 mg [blank = off] ·
Switches to a different n once the strip you are cutting from drops under 3 mg, where the same percentage is a much smaller piece of film. Setting 10 here makes the back half drop 10% per cycle instead of 16.7%.
Your cutting tolerance (mm) [0.5] ·
How accurately you can cut. Half a millimetre is realistic with a razor and a steel ruler. It feeds the "cutting error vs the step" graph, and it sets how much simplicity the life-size film's Folding mode may buy: a fold no further out than your own slip is offered in place of a closer but fiddlier one. Among folds that take the same strokes, it still takes the closer dose. It never changes the schedule. Set it to 0 to hide that graph and to always take the closest fold.
Half-life for the lag curve (h) [32] ·
Buprenorphine's half-life, usually quoted as 24-42 hours for a film. Draws the dashed lag curve on the first graph and nothing else. In that film range the curve tracks the ladder with a few days' lag, which is the point of it. A long value (hundreds or thousands of hours, the depot picture) stays high and only slowly falls toward the new dose, because each day's intake is a small slice of what is already on board. Set it to 0 to hide the curve. 2160 h is 90 days, enough to sketch a 60-day injection tail.
Day 1 date [blank = no calendar] ·
The date you plan to start. Fills in the Calendar panel with real dates and marks today, so you can see which cycle you are on. Leave blank and the plan stays in day numbers.
Switch to 2 mg films at or below 2.25 mg [on] ·
When the strip you cut from reaches 2.25 mg, restart on a 2 mg film. A 2 mg film is a quarter the density, so the same dose is four times longer and the same cut is four times more forgiving. Turn it off to stay on one strength the whole way.
Show n = 6 / 8 / 10 comparison [on] ·
Adds the panel and graph comparing three taper speeds side by side, so you can see what slowing down costs in time and total medication.

Limitation: film strengths

The schedule runs on one film strength at a time (set Start dose, or name it with "Film strength you cut") and then either stays on that strength or switches to 2 mg films. A dose bigger than one film is fine, just several strips of the same strength. It does not auto-step 12 → 8 → 4 → 2. Clicking 4 mg or 12 mg in the size table only changes the life-size drawing, not the taper math. To plan a different start, change the inputs and let the tool recalc. Try Start dose 12, or Start dose 4, or uncheck the 2 mg switch to stay on 8 mg films the whole way.

The method, every day of a cycle

  1. Start the cycle with a piece that is the current "whole strip" (cycle 1: a full 8 mg film, or several if your dose is bigger than one film).
  2. Keep the full width. Cut only along the length. Mark, then cut with a fresh razor on a mat, not scissors. (The film panel's Folding mode is the one exception, and it draws both cuts for you.)
  3. Cut 1/n off the right end. That short sliver goes in the save jar. Take the long left piece. Once daily is enough. If the day is more than one film, take the whole ones as they are and mark only the last: one cut a day, whatever the dose.
  4. In practice you open a fresh film each day rather than keeping yesterday's piece, so measure the same cut from the other end: mark Take mm from the left, and everything right of that mark, Save mm, goes in the jar. It is the same cut. From cycle 2 on, the save is larger than the sliver, because it also carries the part earlier cycles had already taken off; it grows every cycle, and that growth is the Δ save column, which is the sliver.
  5. Do that for n days. The save jar now holds one full piece of this cycle's size. That is a buffer / safety net, not extra daily dose. If you eat the bank, the dose never drops.
  6. Next cycle, the leftover size is the new whole strip. In the default geometric mode that is dose × (1 − 1/n) and the cut shrinks with it; in linear mode you cut the identical piece again, so the dose steps down by the same milligrams every time. Repeat until you hit the target, or in linear mode until there is nothing left to cut.

If it gets rough

  1. Hold: stay at this cycle's daily dose for another n days. Cravings, bad sleep, restlessness, sweats and GI upset are all data, cravings most of all. Holding is not failure.
  2. Optional hedges: stretch each cycle to 8-9 days (same 1/n cut, more settling time), or switch n to 10 once you are below 3 mg.
  3. When the sliver is under ~1 mm, stop shaving 8 mg films. Switch to 2 mg films, which happens by default once the strip reaches 2.25 mg, so the same cut is four times more forgiving.
  4. Store saved pieces airtight, dry, separated by wax paper, and locked up. Ask the prescriber to step the monthly quantity down; return surplus via pharmacy take-back.

Compare n = 6 / 8 / 10

8 mg films all the way; last cycle still above 1 mg. The save jar is not fed back into the daily dose. Slower is not free: n = 10 uses about 3× the medication of n = 6 because you spend about 3× longer on the ladder.

Prescription quantity

Ingested-equivalent strips per 30 days. If the prescription stays at 30 strips a month, the surplus is a stockpile, which is a diversion risk and a household hazard. Ask the prescriber to step quantity down with the dose, and return surplus through a pharmacy take-back.

Notes that make or break this

  • When you measure, cut along one axis only. Keep the full width and only shorten the length, so length fraction = dose fraction and a single number describes the cut. The film's Folding mode does cut both axes, and the arithmetic still holds there because area fraction is dose fraction on an evenly made sheet; what it gives up is the single measurement, which is the point of it. What you must not do is cut both axes while thinking in lengths.
  • Use a fresh razor/craft blade and a ruler on a clean mat, not scissors. Mark before you cut. Cutting films is not manufacturer-sanctioned; the drug is distributed reasonably evenly, which is why this method is widely used.
  • Cutting aids. A steel ruler and a fresh blade on a cutting mat is enough for most of the ladder. If your cuts wander, small purpose-made film slicers exist. Search for "Suboxone film cutter" or "subslicer". A guided cut you can repeat beats a freehand one you cannot.
  • Doses below what you can cut. Some people move to liquid (volumetric) dosing at the very bottom: dissolve a film in a measured volume of water and draw a dose with an oral syringe. A 2 mg film in 20 mL is theoretically 0.1 mg/mL. Treat that number as arithmetic, not as a measurement. It is not manufacturer-sanctioned (the labelling says take the film whole), buprenorphine is only sparingly water-soluble so the real concentration can differ from the calculated one, a homemade solution is not sterile, and you only absorb what you hold under the tongue. Swallowed, most of it is lost. Raise it with your prescriber before trying it. There may be a better route: quartered 2 mg films, a different product, or a properly compounded liquid.
  • Saved slivers are surplus / a safety net. Do not add them back into the daily dose or the taper never drops. Store airtight and dry, separated by wax paper, and keep them locked up.
  • The low end is where 8 mg films fail. Ask your prescriber for 2 mg films before you need them (around the time daily dose is heading through ~4 mg toward ~2 mg). A 2 mg film is one quarter the density of an 8 mg one, so the same dose is four times longer and the same cut is four times more forgiving.
  • Holding is not failure. If a cycle leaves you with bad sleep, restlessness, sweats, GI upset, or above all a spike in cravings, stay at that dose for another cycle or two before dropping again.
  • The two cut modes trade different things. Geometric holds the percentage step constant and lets the milligrams shrink. That is the gentler shape at the low end, but it never reaches zero. Linear holds the milligrams constant and lets the percentage grow. It reaches zero on a schedule, but the last steps are the biggest ones you will have taken. Neither is the right answer for everyone; both let you hold a cycle whenever you need to, and holding is never a failure.
  • Linear is the easier one to cut. The mark never moves, so you measure once and reuse it every day instead of working out a new, smaller measurement each cycle, and it never shrinks under a millimetre, where a razor and a ruler stop resolving. That is also why this page ignores the 2 mg switch in linear mode: the switch exists to rescue a cut that has got too fine, and here it never does.
  • Once daily is fine at every level here. Buprenorphine's half-life is long enough that splitting doses buys you nothing.
  • Every step is held for days on purpose. That same long half-life means a new dose needs most of a cycle to land: about 88-91% of the way there on day 1, and 98-100% by day 6, across the usual 24-42 hour range. It is why the ladder moves in cycles instead of shaving a little off daily, and it puts a floor under how fast this can honestly go: the default is roughly two months from 8 mg to 1 mg. Plenty of people need longer than that, and the tool is built for it. Hold a cycle, raise n, or stretch the cycle. n = 10 on 9-day cycles is about six months. Going slower is a plan, not a failure.
  • n = 6 (16.7% every 6 days) is at the fast end of typical 10-25% steps. You may not feel a drop until day 4 or 5, right as you are about to drop again. Hedge by stretching the cycle to 8-9 days (same cut, more settling time) or switch to n = 10 below 3 mg.
  • The schedule starts from one given film size and either stays there or switches only to 2 mg films. It will not auto-change 12 → 8 → 4 mg. Clicking those rows only redraws the strip. Change Start dose / strip strength and the tool recalculates the whole ladder.
  • Ask about the long-acting injections. Sublocade (monthly) and Brixadi (weekly or monthly) are buprenorphine, the same active drug as your film without the naloxone, injected under the skin by a clinician, where it forms a depot that releases for weeks. Two things make them worth raising here. First, you do not have to reach the bottom of this ladder before switching: both are labelled for a direct transfer from an established daily sublingual dose. Second, after the last injection the drug leaves over months rather than days. The prescribing information puts the terminal half-life at roughly 43-60 days for Sublocade and 19-26 days for monthly Brixadi, against the 24-42 hours this page assumes for a film. That is why people describe the ending as tapering itself. That is a fair reading of the pharmacology, but neither product is approved as a taper, and withdrawal can still arrive, sometimes weeks or months after the final shot, so it needs watching for longer than you would expect. The two differ in a way that matters to this goal: Brixadi has more strengths, so a prescriber can step you further down before stopping, while Sublocade has the longer tail once you do. Do not read the injection's milligrams against your film's. Different route, different absorption; Sublocade's 100 mg monthly maintenance dose sits near a 24 mg/day sublingual blood level, not a 100 mg one. Both are supplied under restricted distribution and given in a clinic rather than collected from a pharmacy, so ask whether an office actually administers them, not just whether it prescribes buprenorphine. This is a conversation to have with a prescriber; it is not a plan you can make from this page.
  • Bring this schedule to your prescriber. A taper from 8 mg to 1-2 mg is a routine, well-supported goal, and they are the one who can write 2 mg films and match quantity to the ladder.
  • Strictly unofficially, the method can be called SAS-Sub-minning, because the stat you are grinding down is Suboxone.
