SAS-Taper

GitHub: bhbmaster/SAS-Taper

SAS means save-a-sliver. Each day you cut 1/n off the current piece, save that sliver, and take the rest — treating what remains as a complete strip. After n days the save jar holds one full piece: a buffer, not extra dose. Numbers update as you change the inputs.

Not medical advice. This is a calculator for a plan to take to your prescriber. Buprenorphine pieces are severely dangerous to children and pets — keep saved slivers locked up. Holding a cycle is fine; cravings are the signal that matters most.

How to use this

Why save-a-sliver

The demanding part of a taper is often not the milligrams but the sense of getting less. SAS frames each day’s remaining piece as a whole strip — the dose you have — while the cut-off sliver leaves the daily routine. People tend to work with what is in hand. Surplus that stays in view is easier to use; once the sliver is saved, it is no longer part of today’s dose. The bank is a safety net if you need to step back up, not a second supply for the same day.

Instructions

  1. Use the calculator on this page, or run the same math in a terminal with python3 taper.py from the repo. To keep a copy offline, save this page — it is one self-contained file and needs no connection.
  2. Measure your film with a ruler along its length only — the 22 mm side, the one you will be cutting. Put that number in “Start film length.” All four Suboxone strengths are 22 mm on that side, so it is the same number whichever you start on. Default n = 6 (16.7% every 6 days). If you want a gentler taper, raise n to 8 or 10.
  3. The cards, graphs, and schedule recalculate live. Click a cycle row (or a graph step) to see that day’s cut mark and a life-size film: TAKE on the left, SAVE on the right. The cut-mark bar is a full unused film; later cycles hash the rest that is already off.
  4. If your dose is bigger than one film — 16 mg on 8 mg strips, say — the day is simply several strips. The Film column shows ×2, and only one of those strips is ever cut: take the whole ones as they are.
  5. Print / save as PDF and take it to your prescriber before day 1 so they can match quantity and write 2 mg films for the back half.

What each input does

Defaults in brackets. Only the first three usually need touching; everything else has a sensible default.

Start dose (mg) [8] —
What you take daily now, before the taper begins. It also decides which film strength the plan is built on — 2, 4, 8 or 12 mg, whichever is the smallest that holds it. Above 12 mg no single film is big enough, so the day becomes several strips: 16 mg is two 8 mg films, and only one of them gets cut.
n (days & cut) [6] —
Does two jobs at once, which is the whole trick: you cut 1/n off the piece each day, and each cycle lasts n days. After n days the saved slivers add up to one whole piece. n = 6 drops 16.7% per cycle; n = 8 drops 12.5%; n = 10 drops 10%. Bigger n means a gentler, slower taper.
Start film length (mm) [22] —
Measure your own film along the side you will cut and put that number here. All four Suboxone strengths are 22 mm on that side, so the default is usually right — measure anyway, since every cut mark on the page is derived from it.
2 mg film length (mm) [22] —
The same measurement for the 2 mg film, used after the switch. Ignored if you turn the switch off.
Target (mg/day) [1] —
Where the ladder stops. The run ends on the first cycle at or below this dose. Must be lower than the start dose.
Strip strength (mg) [8] —
The strength of one film as dispensed. Used only to convert milligrams into “how many strips”, for the totals and the prescription-quantity table. It does not change the ladder.
Film strength you cut (mg) [blank = auto] —
Which strength of film you actually hold. Left blank the tool picks the smallest official size that fits the start dose — 2, 4, 8 or 12 mg — and 8 mg for a start above 12 mg, where no single film holds the dose. Set it if you have something else: a 16 mg start is two 8 mg strips a day, or two 12 mg strips if that is what is in the box.
Hold / stretch days [blank = same as n] —
Makes every cycle longer without changing the size of the cut. Set 9 with n = 6 and you still cut 1/6, but hold each dose for 9 days instead of 6 — the same taper, more settling time between drops.
n below 3 mg [blank = off] —
Switches to a different n once the strip you are cutting from drops under 3 mg, where the same percentage is a much smaller piece of film. Setting 10 here makes the back half drop 10% per cycle instead of 16.7%.
Your cutting tolerance (mm) [0.5] —
How accurately you can actually cut — half a millimetre is realistic with a razor and a steel ruler. Feeds the “cutting error vs the step” graph only; it never changes the schedule. Set it to 0 to hide that graph.
Half-life for the lag curve (h) [32] —
Buprenorphine's half-life, usually quoted as 24–42 hours. Draws the dashed lag curve on the first graph and nothing else. Set it to 0 to hide the curve.
Day 1 date [blank = no calendar] —
The date you plan to start. Fills in the Calendar panel with real dates and marks today, so you can see which cycle you are on. Leave blank and the plan stays in day numbers.
Switch to 2 mg films at or below 2.25 mg [on] —
When the strip you cut from reaches 2.25 mg, restart on a 2 mg film. A 2 mg film is a quarter the density, so the same dose is four times longer and the same cut is four times more forgiving. Turn it off to stay on one strength the whole way.
Show n = 6 / 8 / 10 comparison [on] —
Adds the panel and graph comparing three taper speeds side by side, so you can see what slowing down costs in time and total medication.

Limitation — film strengths

The schedule runs on one film strength at a time (set Start dose, or name it with “Film strength you cut”) and then either stays on that strength or switches to 2 mg films. A dose bigger than one film is fine — that is just several strips of the same strength. It does not auto-step 12 → 8 → 4 → 2. Clicking 4 mg or 12 mg in the size table only changes the life-size drawing, not the taper math. To plan a different start, change the inputs and let the tool recalc — for example Start dose 12, or Start dose 4, or uncheck the 2 mg switch to stay on 8 mg films the whole way.

The method — every day of a cycle

  1. Start the cycle with a piece that is the current “whole strip” (cycle 1: a full 8 mg film — or several, if your dose is bigger than one film).
  2. Keep the full width. Cut only along the length. Mark, then cut with a fresh razor on a mat — not scissors.
  3. Cut 1/n off the right end. That short sliver goes in the save jar. Take the long left piece. Once daily is enough. If the day is more than one film, cut the sliver off one of them and take the others whole — never split the sliver across two strips.
  4. Do that for n days. The save jar now holds one full piece of this cycle’s size. That is a buffer / safety net, not extra daily dose. If you eat the bank, the dose never drops.
  5. Next cycle, the leftover size is the new whole strip: dose × (1 − 1/n). Repeat until you hit the target (default 1 mg).

If it gets rough

  1. Hold: stay at this cycle’s daily dose for another n days. Cravings, bad sleep, restlessness, sweats, or GI upset are data — especially cravings. Holding is not failure.
  2. Optional hedges: stretch each cycle to 8–9 days (same 1/n cut, more settling time), or switch n to 10 once you are below 3 mg.
  3. When the sliver is under ~1 mm, stop shaving 8 mg films. Switch to 2 mg films — on by default once the strip reaches 2.25 mg — so the same cut is four times more forgiving.
  4. Store saved pieces airtight, dry, separated by wax paper, and locked up. Ask the prescriber to step the monthly quantity down; return surplus via pharmacy take-back.

Prescription quantity

Ingested-equivalent strips per 30 days. If the prescription stays at 30 strips a month, the surplus is a stockpile — a diversion risk and a household hazard. Ask the prescriber to step quantity down with the dose, and return surplus through a pharmacy take-back.

Notes that make or break this

  • Cut along one axis only. Keep the full width; only shorten the length. Then length fraction = dose fraction. Cutting both dimensions loses the arithmetic.
  • Use a fresh razor/craft blade and a ruler on a clean mat, not scissors. Mark before you cut. Cutting films is not manufacturer-sanctioned; the drug is distributed reasonably evenly, which is why this method is widely used.
  • Saved slivers are surplus / a safety net. Do not add them back into the daily dose or the taper never drops. Store airtight and dry, separated by wax paper, and keep them locked up.
  • The low end is where 8 mg films fail. Ask your prescriber for 2 mg films before you need them (around the time daily dose is heading through ~4 mg toward ~2 mg). A 2 mg film is one quarter the density of an 8 mg one, so the same dose is four times longer and the same cut is four times more forgiving.
  • Holding is not failure. If a cycle leaves you with bad sleep, restlessness, sweats, GI upset, or — most importantly — a spike in cravings, stay at that dose for another cycle or two before dropping again.
  • Once daily is fine at every level here. Buprenorphine’s half-life is long enough that splitting doses buys you nothing.
  • Every step is held for days on purpose. That same long half-life means a new dose needs most of a cycle to fully land — about 88–91% of the way there on day 1 and 98–100% by day 6, across the usual 24–42 hour range. It is why the ladder moves in cycles instead of shaving a little off daily, and it puts a floor under how fast this can honestly go: the default is roughly two months from 8 mg to 1 mg. Plenty of people need longer than that, and the tool is built for it — hold a cycle, raise n, or stretch the cycle. n = 10 on 9-day cycles is about six months. Going slower is a plan, not a failure.
  • n = 6 (16.7% every 6 days) is at the fast end of typical 10–25% steps. You may not feel a drop until day 4 or 5, right as you are about to drop again. Hedge by stretching the cycle to 8–9 days (same cut, more settling time) or switch to n = 10 below 3 mg.
  • The schedule starts from one given film size and either stays there or switches only to 2 mg films. It will not auto-change 12 → 8 → 4 mg. Clicking those rows only redraws the strip. Change Start dose / strip strength and the tool recalculates the whole ladder.
  • Bring this schedule to your prescriber. A taper from 8 mg to 1–2 mg is a routine, well-supported goal, and they are the one who can write 2 mg films and match quantity to the ladder.
  • Strictly unofficially, the method can be called SAS-Sub-minning — the stat you are grinding down is Suboxone.
